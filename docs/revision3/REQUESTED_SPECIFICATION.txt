## 6. Keep statistical claims valid on real observations

Separate three evidence types:
A. Natural year/cohort comparisons: actual periods and unaltered released observations.
B. Resampling/replay checks: original records sampled under explicitly constructed conditions.
C. Existing mathematical simulations: controlled ground-truth validation.

Make A the main new evidence. Do not combine their denominators or call all three real-world deployments. In A, true drift onset, affected group, and no-drift status are generally unknown. Report observed changes, disagreement, coverage, and outcome associations. Do not call every year transition a true drift or report detection accuracy without external ground truth. An outcome audit is not a perfect ground-truth drift detector.

Use label-blind input monitoring and independent, prespecified outcome evaluation. Keep actual subgroup TPR/FPR/accuracy and denominators alongside disparities. Pre-existing performance gaps and changes in those gaps are different. Do not infer discrimination, causality, or bias removal from input alerts.

Preserve plus-one p-values and conservative stopping. Never reuse a fresh 5% budget indefinitely or treat the union of several separately controlled monitors as one 5%-controlled system.

Same-period randomized controls are constructed checks, not certified naturally stationary periods.
