# What the corrected charts mean

AI-assisted explanations for review, not a final submission written as the student. Charts 1–5 use genuine OULAD records. Charts 6–10 use the original Version 2 **mathematical simulations**. The existing ACS observations and real-record resampling controls are separate, unchanged evidence.

**PSI** compares the proportions falling into fixed bins. **KS** compares the cumulative distributions of the original numerical values. Their numerical scales differ. A larger number means a larger measured difference by that statistic; it does not automatically mean unfairness, prediction failure, statistical significance or a known real drift event.

**Accumulation** waits until a group has enough current records, then tests the entire collected block. Both methods receive that same block. Waiting provides more data but can delay a result and mix older and newer observations. Enough current data does not fix an undersized historical reference.

1. **Historical bin correction.** The old bins placed every valid previous-attempt count in one bin. The new bins show zero, one and two-or-more attempts separately. The choice uses historical design records only. The 2+ bin contains only 13 design students, so its size remains a limitation. This is a repair after reviewing results.
2. **Corrected OULAD distances.** Gray squares are original PSI and blue circles are corrected PSI. Orange KS points stay unchanged. Missing results are explicitly labelled with their small reference counts; there are still only four supported views. A changed PSI after repairing its bins is not proof of higher detection accuracy.
3. **Prediction baseline.** The blue model is the original, frozen logistic predictor. Orange always predicts Pass/Distinction because that was the most common training label. Accuracy is the share of correct predictions. TPR is the share of actual Pass/Distinction records predicted positive. FPR is the share of actual Fail/Withdrawn records incorrectly predicted positive. The orange model gets every positive right and every negative wrong. The blue model's later accuracy is slightly below this simple baseline; show that context.
4. **Outcome gaps.** Compare a group's prediction rate with everyone outside that group, then compare that difference between cohorts. Gray crosses mean the required label counts were too small; they are not zero changes. Groups overlap, and these are descriptive comparisons. The constant model's zero TPR/FPR gaps come from predicting everybody positive, not from proving fairness.
5. **Inputs and outcomes together.** Each point pairs a group's monitored PSI with a separately measured accuracy-gap change. This small display cannot establish a causal relationship or a dependable correlation. Every unsupported pairing remains in the accompanying table.
6. **All original synthetic grid cells.** Every square shows how many of 40 simulated streams raised any post-change alert. Rows vary the affected-group size; columns vary the introduced shift. Both statistics get the same records and collection rules. All cells are shown, including ties and weaker results.
7. **Synthetic detection and false alerts.** Left: how often a wholly unchanged stream triggers any alarm. Right: how often a shifted stream triggers a post-change alarm. The 5% line is the design budget, not an empirical guarantee. An alarm anywhere is different from identifying the exact affected subgroup. Error bars describe simulation uncertainty under the specified benchmark, not real deployment uncertainty.
8. **Target coverage and waiting.** This revisits the precise 1.4-SD examples behind the earlier Version 2 claim, retaining all original group shares and both batch sizes. Coverage tells whether the true affected group was tested. Delay includes failures by assigning them the 15-batch post-change horizon; it does not average only the successful runs. Shared collection makes support curves overlap.
9. **Every cancellation and robustness case.** The additional original cases stay visible. An outcome-only change can hurt predictions without changing monitored inputs. A correlation change can leave each individually monitored variable unchanged. A benign input shift can be visible without harming the predictor. Do not call every alert a fairness violation.
10. **Costs with their limits.** The software measures statistic time on the same blocks. PSI can store counts while KS uses numerical values, but those array sizes do not include the whole program. Shared records, bookkeeping, temporary allocations and reference data also consume memory. Tiny timing differences depend on execution order and the machine. A separate alternating-order cost table helps check this; no universal efficiency claim follows.

Each chart has a CSV of its displayed data, a PDF/SVG for sharp printing and a PNG for slides. Tables retain exact values, support failures and all cases. Confidence intervals are pointwise, not a correction for making many simultaneous claims. A zero-width paired interval can simply mean all observed method differences were identical.

The strongest honest description remains an implemented and reproducible subgroup-monitoring study. The work does not establish a new algorithm, a generally superior PSI statistic, a strong OULAD prediction model, or natural drift-detection accuracy.
